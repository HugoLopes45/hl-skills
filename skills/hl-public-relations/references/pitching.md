# Pitching

## Target types

Build target types before names. For each type, record:

1. **Beat:** the topic the journalist or host currently covers, from pieces the owner read, with the date read.
2. **Reader fit:** the outlet's audience is the one the story is for.
3. **Contact route:** the tip or pitch route the outlet publishes. Do not guess an address; a guessed address is unverified and gets no send.
4. **Angle:** the angle from the story reference that fits this type.

Target tiers:

- **Target tier 1:** a custom pitch that cites a specific recent piece.
- **Target tier 2:** the standard pitch with one tailored line.
- **Target tier 3:** skip unless the owner judges the story exceptional.

Skip a journalist whose beat does not match, who has not published on the beat in the window the owner sets, or who has said they take no pitches.

List a person only when the owner supplies the name and has checked the recent work. This skill does not browse; the owner supplies the list.

Award entries are targets too. Enter only with supplied proof. Check the fees and rules on the organiser's current page; until you read them, mark them `unknown`.

## Pitch core

Write four parts, in this order:

1. **Hook:** one sentence with the specific fact. The subject line names that fact, not the company. Keep it under 50 characters (heuristic default).
2. **Reader reason:** one sentence on what this outlet's readers gain.
3. **Proof:** the document, dataset, or customer, with the link or contact the owner supplies.
4. **Ask:** one ask: an interview, a quote, an embargo, or an exclusive.

Keep the pitch under 150 words (heuristic default). Pitch the story; the product is the proof. Send to one journalist at a time.

Pitch killers:

- The subject names the company but not the fact.
- No proof is attached or linked.
- The key facts sit in an attached file, not in the body.
- A quote has no approval record.
- The pitch could go unchanged to a different outlet.
- The pitch asks the journalist to sign something before a call.

## Timing

- **Open release:** the default. Every target gets the story at the same time.
- **Exclusive:** one outlet gets the story first. Use it for the strongest story only, state its scope ("exclusive to one outlet in one market"), and plan the release for the next day (heuristic default).
- **Embargo:** a time before which the journalist may not publish. Offer it only to a journalist who has honored one before. State the day, time, and time zone.

If an embargo is broken, stop pitching that journalist.

## Follow-up

- **Day 0:** the pitch.
- **Day 3 at most (heuristic default):** only with fresh information, such as another fact or a customer who agreed to talk. Without it, do not send.
- **Day 7 (heuristic default):** the last message, with a fresh hook.
- **After day 7:** stop. Pitch again only with a different story.

Never send "did you see my email", and never bump a pitch without further content.
