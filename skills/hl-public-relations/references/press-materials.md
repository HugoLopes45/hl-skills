# Press Materials

## Press release

Write one only for an announcement that outlets need on record. Pitch first; the release follows the pitch.

1. **Headline:** the fact in one line.
2. **Dateline:** the place and `[PLACEHOLDER: date]`.
3. **Lead:** who, what, when, where, and why a reader cares, in one paragraph.
4. **Body:** two to four short paragraphs (heuristic default) of supplied facts, each with its source tag.
5. **Quote:** the approved exact words, with the role and the approval record.
6. **Company description:** the supplied boilerplate.
7. **Press contact:** a name and an email address the owner supplies.

Put the key facts in the body, not in an attachment.

## Press page

Include only what the owner supplies, and only what exists:

- A one-paragraph company description, ready to paste.
- Bios of spokespeople, each approved by that person.
- Logo files and usage rules the owner holds.
- Product screenshots the owner has cleared for publication.
- Recent coverage as links to published pieces. Use an outlet's logo only where its rules allow; check the outlet's current rule.
- Founding date, team size, and funding, only where the owner discloses them.
- A press email address, not a form.
- Recent announcements, dated.

Add a response-time line only with the owner's commitment: `[PLACEHOLDER: response time the owner commits to]`.

## Inbound press requests

Reply only when the owner has direct experience or data on the question.

- Reply before the deadline the request states.
- Keep the reply under 200 words (heuristic default).
- Order: one credential sentence, the answer in two sentences, one specific supplied example, the approved exact quote or a `[PLACEHOLDER: quote approved by ...]` slot, and the attribution the owner approved.
- No product pitch. One reply per company for each request.

## Podcast guest brief

Inputs: the show's episode list or notes the owner supplies, and the guest's stories with their supplied numbers.

1. List the topics that recur across the supplied episodes.
2. Map each guest story to one recurring topic.
3. Pick two or three stories whose numbers the guest can say aloud with their source.
4. Write the brief: the show's focus, its recurring topics, the mapped stories, and what the owner must still supply.

Use only the show's public material about its hosts.

## Coverage corrections

Ask a publisher to replace a repeated criticism with a different true statement only when the criticism is wrong. Never ask for removal of a true criticism. Draft the request with the supplied fact and its source tag; the owner sends it.

## Measurement

Track each measure with its source and date range:

- Coverage count per month, by outlet.
- Visits that arrive from coverage links.
- Change in branded search, from the owner's analytics.
- Sales conversations in which a prospect cites a piece, from the owner's notes.
- Whether answer tools cite the coverage, checked by hand on a dated query.

Do not use advertising value equivalency. It prices coverage without proof that anyone acted on it.

Set no target without the owner's baseline; write `Default: none` for the target.
