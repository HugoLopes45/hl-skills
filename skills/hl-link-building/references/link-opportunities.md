# Link Opportunities

Read this file when choosing prospect types, finding prospects, checking an opportunity, judging a vendor offer, drafting outreach, or listing questions for the owner's adviser.

## Prospect types

| Prospect type | Why it would link | Asset or angle |
|---|---|---|
| Sites that mention the brand or its data without linking | The writer already chose the brand; the ask is one link | The exact page the mention should link to |
| Sites linking to a dead resource that the asset replaces | The readers lost a useful reference | The asset, if it covers what the dead page covered |
| Resource or tools pages on the topic | The list needs one tool or guide per topic | A free tool, template, or guide, not the homepage |
| Best-of and comparison pages that buyers read | Buyers choose from that list | An honest evaluation: who the product fits, its price, a trial |
| Partner, integration, and customer pages | The partner gains by listing the product | The integration page, a case study, or a listing |
| Editorial sites that take expert articles | Their readers need that expertise | One article on a gap the site has, with a plain author bio link |

## Finding prospects

Searches and AI answer tools need web access. Without it, mark each search `not measured`, ask the owner for its results, and do not guess pages.

- Mentions: search the brand name or data title in quotes, excluding your own domain. Keep the pages that do not link to you.
- Dead resources: find outbound links that return errors on relevant pages, and compare what the old page covered.
- Rival links: list the referring sites of the pages that outrank the goal page. A site that links to two or more rivals but not to you is a warm prospect; two is a heuristic default.
- Lists: search the category's best-of, alternatives, and comparison queries, and put the same questions to AI answer tools. Record each cited page.
- Relationships: partners, integrations, customers, vendors, and event organizers from the owner's records.
- Qualify each prospect with the checks below, never with an authority score alone.

## Opportunity checks

Record each check as `pass`, `fail`, or `unknown`. `unknown` is not a pass.

1. **Relevance.** The site's readers need what the goal page offers. Topic fit outweighs any authority score.
2. **Editorial standards.** Named authors, an editor who reviews submissions, and outbound links that relate to the topic. Fail: any topic accepted for a fee, or outbound links that are mostly unrelated commercial links.
3. **Reachable owner.** A named person owns the page. Fail: only a generic contact form.
4. **Value to the goal page.** The link lands on a page that needs authority, not only the homepage. Fail: the only offer is a homepage link.
5. **Placement type.** Editorial, or paid or product-for-review. A paid or product-for-review link passes only with a `rel="sponsored"` or `rel="nofollow"` value and a visible disclosure, as the Refusals in SKILL.md require. Check the live anchor's `rel` value after publication; the observed value counts, not the agreed one.

Before you call any placement compliant, check the search engine's current official link and spam documents. Write `unverified` for a document you did not read in this session.

## Vendor checks

Record each vendor check as `pass`, `fail`, or `unknown`. A `fail` declines the offer, with verdict `Stopped`. An `unknown` gives verdict `Draft`, with the missing fact in Next. When every check passes, judge each placement with the opportunity checks.

1. Does the vendor sell followed links by price per link or by authority score? That sells followed links for their ranking credit, which the Refusals in SKILL.md decline. Fail.
2. Can the vendor show the live pages, with their named authors? A list of sites that publish anything for a fee is a network. Fail.
3. Do the sites accept any topic, or carry unrelated commercial outbound links? Fail.
4. Does the vendor choose exact-match anchor text for you? Fail.
5. What does the vendor report? Link counts and authority scores alone, with no placements, rankings, or traffic, mean the links are the product. Fail.

A legitimate vendor sells work: content, outreach, and placements it does not guarantee.

## Outreach rules

- Lead with the prospect's page and one specific detail on it. If no detail exists, the prospect is not qualified.
- Make one ask: a link, an update, or an evaluation.
- Give the exact URL to link to. For a fix, give the exact place on the page.
- Offer something real when you have it: a data point, a fixed broken link, an expert comment, or an account to evaluate. An account to evaluate is a product-for-review placement: offer it only when the draft asks for `rel="sponsored"` or `rel="nofollow"` and a visible disclosure; the Placement type check stays `unknown` until the live link is read. Never offer money for a followed link.
- Do not name the anchor text. The linking party chooses it.
- Keep the message short. Under 100 words is a heuristic default, not a rule.
- Send one follow-up at most, then stop.
- Write each draft for its page. A template sent unchanged reads as a template.

## Questions for the owner's adviser

List these for the owner's adviser. Do not answer them.

- Disclosure wording and advertising rules for paid or product-for-review placements, for each market.
- Whether each prospect's market allows cold email to that person without prior consent, and what permission record to keep.
- Terms of any partner or vendor contract that grants a link or a listing.
