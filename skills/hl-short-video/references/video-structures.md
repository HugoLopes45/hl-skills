# Video structures

Pick the structure from the viewer's situation and the supplied material. A structure gives the beat order only; the brief sets the length, and the lines come from supplied facts.

- Problem and solution: the problem in the viewer's words, the supported fix, then the next action. Use when a supplied demonstration solves one named problem.
- List: one supported item per beat, each with its proof. Use when the supplied material has several checked items.
- Tutorial: the goal, then each step in order, shown on screen. Use when the steps are supplied and checked.
- Story arc: the setup, the attempt, the result, the lesson. Rule: the story needs supplied first-person experience.
- Point of view: a specific viewer situation in the on-screen text, with the payoff in the last beat. Rule: name a situation only this product's buyer has.
- Reaction with demo: a reaction clip with its hook line, then a hard cut to the demo. Rule: the reaction must be supplied and real; a reaction to another creator's footage needs that footage's rights cleared.
- Silent split-screen tutorial: no spoken lines; on-screen step text carries the steps. Rule: no spoken lines means no subtitles.
- Investigation: a question, the supplied checks or visits, and the finding. Rule: report only supplied findings; never present a staged visit as real.
- Expert on camera: a real expert states a supplied point in their field. Rule: use the expert's supplied credential and approved words; health, medical and financial claims need their supporting evidence.
- Chat reveal: a two-person text exchange; the first image is the hook, and the brand appears only after a peer asks. Heuristic default: 8 to 14 messages. Rule: never present the exchange as a real customer or AI output; every claim in it needs its evidence.
- Underdog: name the incumbent or broken norm, then the brand's fight against it. Rule: the antagonist must be real.
- Education-first: the problem, the mechanism, the proof, then the offer; use when the buyer must understand the mechanism before buying. Rule: the mechanism and proof need supplied support.
- Commentary over imagery: the speaker explains each beat over the supplied image it refers to. Rule: the image must be supplied or its rights cleared.
- Conversation: two people in an exchange; the product enters through an objection the exchange answers. Rule: a scripted exchange is labelled as scripted, never presented as candid.
- Sound-led: no spoken lines; the sound of the real product in use carries the piece. Rule: capture that sound from the real product.
- Street interview: the question, each supplied answer from real passers-by who consent, then the takeaway. Rule: answers are supplied; a recreated interview never presents as candid.
- Math: the fixed budget, then each part of it in turn. Rule: use supplied numbers only, each with its source.
- Niche guide: the place, then the expert's view of each part of it, filmed there. Rule: the expert and the place must be supplied.
- Shiny object: a visually novel process or place the speaker can really show, one stage at a time. Rule: show only access the speaker has.
