# Hook patterns

Use when a hook is requested. Pick a pattern the supplied material supports; each pattern needs its payoff later in the video.

## Writing a hook set

1. Fix the viewer segment: one shared situation from supplied material. Do not invent a segment.
2. Fix the motivation: the pain, desire or objection in the viewer's own words, quoted verbatim from supplied reviews, comments or sales notes. If none is supplied, write the hook from the problem the brief states, never as a quote, and add `[PLACEHOLDER: viewer wording needed]`.
3. Fix the format: a face to camera, a screen recording, a reply to a comment or another delivery. Choose it before writing the line.
4. Write the requested number of hooks for that segment, motivation and format, built as the Hook check in [video-method.md](video-method.md) describes.
5. Vary the pattern across a batch. Rewordings of one pattern count as one pattern.

## Patterns

- **Curiosity gap.** Withhold the key noun; the video reveals it. Rule: the reveal must pay off in the video.
- **Bold claim.** A specific, checkable statement. Rule: show its proof on screen or in the next beat.
- **Confession.** "I was about to..." from a real person's own experience. Rule: never an AI presenter as a customer.
- **Myth or tip.** "I never..." or "Don't..." from a supplied expert or the owner, then the right way. Rule: the right way must be supplied.
- **Situation.** "If you're..." naming a situation the viewer recognizes. Rule: name a situation, never a personal attribute.
- **Question.** The exact question the buyer asks, in the buyer's words. Rule: take the wording from a supplied source.
- **Choice.** Two options the viewer already weighs. Rule: both options are real, and the video answers which one.
- **Old habit against change.** A habit or state the viewer knows, set against a change. Rule: the payoff takes a side, and the change is visually honest and typical.
- **Us vs them.** A behavior most people recognize. Rule: any rival claim needs its source.
- **False assumption.** A belief the viewer holds, challenged. Rule: keep the body fair to the other option.
- **Proof first.** The result, shown in the first beat. Rule: show real proof only.
- **Result quote.** A supplied review. Rule: quote it word for word, with the reviewer's permission beyond its public post.
- **Velocity stat.** A counted, current number. Rule: keep its source and date.
- **Timely event.** A dated event the viewer lives through this season. Rule: retire the hook when the date ends.
- **Dare.** A challenge the product can meet. Rule: back it with an offer the business already makes.
- **Point of view.** A specific everyday moment, shot from the viewer's view or the creator's phone. Rule: name the situation as the Point of view structure in [video-structures.md](video-structures.md) does.
- **Reply to a comment.** A real comment, shown as written. Rule: blur the commenter unless they agree.
- **Mid-sentence start.** A spoken thought already in motion; the set-up breath is cut. Rule: the first subtitle shows the first words.
