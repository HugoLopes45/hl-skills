# Proof and claims

Use this file before you write any factual line.

## Claim basis

| Basis | Action |
|---|---|
| Supplied in the brief | Write it with its own limits. |
| Read in a checked source, with its link | Write it, and name the link in Next. |
| Neither, and the user asked for the claim or the reader cannot act without the fact | Write `[PLACEHOLDER: proof needed]`, naming the proof that replaces it. |
| Neither, and the piece works without it | Omit it. Name it once in Next with what it would add. |
| Not needed for the reader's decision | Omit it. |

## Exact grade

- Keep each supplied fact at the strength it was supplied. "22 technicians" never becomes "22 licensed technicians"; "most jobs" never becomes "every job"; "4.8" never becomes "nearly 5".
- A test run once, by the maker, is written as that: who ran it, when, how many times. It does not become "tested" or "proven".
- An average is not a count. Do not state an average as what one reader has.
- Nothing unshipped: no feature, option, policy, or term the business does not have today.
- Arithmetic on supplied numbers is allowed when the sum is shown or obvious and adds no assumption. Anything that needs an assumption is an estimate and shows it.
- A scene or image may dramatize a supplied fact. It may not contain a number, person, event, or result the brief does not hold.

## Thin proof makes quieter copy

Never write a claim bigger than its proof. When the brief has little proof, the copy gets shorter and plainer and leans on what is checkable: the mechanism, the price, the limit, the trial. It does not get a proof section full of slots.

## Never write, unless supplied

- Testimonials, customer names, quotes, star ratings, review counts, user counts, logos, "as seen in" lines, awards, or certifications.
- Guarantees, refunds, delivery or setup times, savings, or results.
- Comparisons with named competitors.

## Declined claims name their unlock

When the user asks for an unsupported claim, do not write it and do not drop it silently. Put one plain line in Next: what was asked, the closest supported line, and the evidence that would unlock it.

Example: "Next: the claim "4.8 stars from 12,000 reviews" needs the export from your review platform, with its date; until then the page shows no rating."

## Testimonials, cases, and reviews

- Keep a supplied quote to the parts it states: the before state, the action, the specific result, the timeframe, and the reaction. Never fill a missing part.
- Headline a case with its starting number and its ending number, each with its unit and period.
- Summarize reviews with the mild criticism included. Name a topic only with a count from the supplied reviews; never invent a percentage.

## Guarantees

- A supplied guarantee becomes one sentence the business would sign: the number, the window, and what happens when it is missed.
- Add one line on what counts toward the guarantee, from the supplied terms; without them, leave a placeholder.
- Replace a vague promise such as "fully satisfied" with the condition it covers, or leave a placeholder.

## Bold ideas

A bold idea is a claim or offer the user states that the business does not do yet. Keep it out of the copy and list it in Next: the idea, the line it would unlock, and what it takes (a build, a policy, a number to check, or the owner's sign-off).

## Numbers

- Check each number's unit, period, and basis at the evidence gate: supplied, measured with a stated method, checked source, or estimated from stated assumptions. Show them in the copy only when the reader needs them.
- Pair each rating with its count and its source: `[rating] from [count] reviews on [platform]`.
- A price from the brief is supplied. A price you derive is estimated; show the assumption beside it.
