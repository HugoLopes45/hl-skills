# Voice and audience

Use this file to choose the register of the copy for the audience the brief names. Apply the same rules to any market, language, or age group.

## Choose the register

1. Collect the words the buyer uses: reviews, support tickets, comments, forum threads, interviews, sales calls. Copy three to five phrases exactly, with their source and locale.
2. Put those phrases into the headline, subhead, and problem bullets where they fit the claim.
3. Without supplied words, write plain, direct sentences. Label the voice `unverified; replace with buyer words`.
4. Write in the language of the brief. Do not translate buyer phrases into a second language unless the brief asks for it.

## Culture-native, not costumed

- Culture-native copy sounds like the buyer's own community: the same formality and the same shared references.
- Do not add slang, memes, emoji, or lowercase styling to signal a generation or a platform. Readers spot imitation, and it lowers trust.
- Use humor only when the product's own situation supplies it, and never at the buyer's expense.
- Use platform jargon only when a supplied source uses it in the same sense.

## Audience labels

- An age group, generation, or platform habit is a hypothesis about a segment, not a fact about one buyer.
- Name a segment only when the brief names it. Confirm the segment with the buyer's own words before you write to it.
- Do not infer where a buyer discovers products, or what they trust, from a segment label. Use a source the user supplied, or write `unverified`.

## Modern agency voice

- Write like a current brand that talks with its audience, not at it: direct, specific, and free of corporate filler. Modern describes the wording, not a generation: do not presume the customer is Gen Z or any other age group.
- Make the reader the subject of the sentence. Show the action they take, not the product's ambition.
- Ask a question only when the reader would ask it. Answer it in the next sentence.

## Creators and community

- Name a creator only with the creator's permission and an approved link. Without both, write `[PLACEHOLDER: creator name and approved post link]`.
- Never write a creator quote, endorsement, or post that the user did not supply.
- Invite a community action (ask a question, post a result, join a thread) only when that community exists. Otherwise use a placeholder for the link.
- A buyer who checks comments or reviews before buying needs proof they can check: a demo, a trial, a named customer quote with permission, or a published policy.

## Proof-led conversion

- Place the call to action next to the strongest supported proof it depends on.
- Replace each adjective with a check the buyer can run: a demo, a trial, a measured result with its method, or a published policy.
