# Owner asks

Use this file when a Next step asks the owner to collect evidence: interviews, a survey, or more sources. The skill never contacts anyone; the owner runs every ask. The evidence rules are in `SKILL.md`.

## Order

- List the public sources the owner can read first: reviews, community threads, tickets already held. They show what to ask and in whose words.
- Pick at least one source per conversation type (see `evidence-reading.md`), up to 8 public sources (heuristic default). Include a competitor review source when the owner names competitors.
- Before writing questions, record each public source's pains, words they use and beliefs, each quoted with its source.
- Then ask for first-party interviews or a survey on what the public sources do not answer.

## Who to ask

- Include people who did not buy, lapsed users, and customers who stopped, not only the best customers. A list of best customers over-represents satisfied users; say so in the Next step when the owner picks from such a list.
- Ask for more sources for a theme only while new sources still add to it. When new sources only repeat a theme, stop and state how many sources it took.
- Close each interview by asking who else to talk to. Record who referred each new contact. Referred contacts skew toward the referrer's circle; say so in the Next step.

## Questions

- Ask open, neutral questions in the respondent's words. Do not lead.
- Each question must be able to return an answer that breaks the card. Rewrite any question that can only confirm the card.
- Tell the respondent the purpose of the conversation or survey. Do not present research as a casual chat.
- For each pain a respondent names, ask what it causes and why that matters, repeating until the answer names a cost, a decision or a stake, or the respondent gives none. Record each answer in order.

## Surveys

- Keep the survey short. Cut any question the card does not need.
- Ask open questions for the buyer's words, not only choice questions.
- For current users, include one question on how much the respondent would miss the product, with graded answers (for example, very disappointed to not disappointed), so answers can be segmented (see `evidence-reading.md`).
- Include one question on who would benefit most and one on the main benefit, in the respondent's words.

## Invitation

- State the time the conversation takes and any incentive the owner sets; record the incentive with the source and screen its quotes as incentivized (see `evidence-reading.md`).
