# Ad creative types

Use this file in draft step 3 of [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) to pick the creative type for a static ad. Each creative type names the proof it needs, from [ad-method.md](ad-method.md). A creative type whose proof the user did not supply becomes a placeholder.

## Choose by awareness stage

- Unaware or problem-aware reader (stages in [ad-method.md](ad-method.md)): use a creative type that gives a stranger a reason to stop with no prior knowledge: the founder message (copy structure 1), a catalog grid (still-image type 7), or a text-on-image statement with its qualifier (still-image type 3).
- Solution-aware, product-aware, or most-aware reader: use a creative type that asks for the action (heuristic default): an offer card (still-image type 2), a review (type 4), a reply to a real comment (type 9), or a before and after rewritten as a process claim (type 5).
- Unknown stage: an offer card (still-image type 2).
- In a batch, name each variant's creative type, awareness stage, and production tier in one line under that variant's heading, by name, not number. The production tier is text only, a supplied photo, or an asset the team must make. Write `stage unknown` where the brief gives no stage. For an asset the team must make, write `capacity unknown` until the user confirms the team can make it, and list that check in Next.

## Still-image types

1. Product hero: one product and one offer. Proof: the offer terms, with currency and end date, from the user.
2. Offer card: the offer terms as the whole message. Proof: the written terms.
3. Text-on-image statement: one claim in the image, with its qualifier in the same line. Proof: the claim's source line.
4. Review static: one review quote. Proof: a dated review the customer lets you quote; otherwise `[PLACEHOLDER: permission to quote]`.
5. Before and after: one visible change, rewritten as a process or capability claim (see the claim types in [ad-method.md](ad-method.md)). Proof: the user's account of the process. A timed or typical result needs a dated case with its method.
6. Us versus them: a comparison of rows. Proof: each row sourced and dated. Name no competitor without a dated source.
7. Catalog grid: several products with their prices. Proof: each price and its currency from the user.
8. Native photo: a photo of real work. Proof: a real photo. A staged scene shown as a real customer photo is a dramatized ad (see the claim types in [ad-method.md](ad-method.md)).
9. Reply to a real comment: the answer to one comment on the user's own page. Proof: the comment, its date, and permission to quote it.
10. Point of view: a first-person line about one specific situation the reader recognizes. Proof: the user's account of that situation. A generic opener is a tell (see [ad-calibration.md](ad-calibration.md)).

## Copy structures for static ads

Fill each slot from supplied facts only. Each slot keeps the proof listed.

1. Founder message: the founder's reason for the business, in the founder's words. Proof: the founder's approved statement.
2. FAQ card: one real customer question in the customer's words, then the answer. Proof: the question's source.
3. Simple guarantee: the guarantee's condition, timeframe, and what the buyer gets back. Proof: the written terms. The business must honor them.
4. With and without: two short lists, what the buyer gets and what the buyer avoids. Proof: each item a supplied fact.
5. Apology letter: one real failure, what changed, and how to reach the business. Proof: the real incident, from the user.
6. Stat callout: one figure with its population, period, and precision. Proof: the documented statistic.

## Carousel

Order the cards as opening line, problem, fix, proof, offer. Each card reads on its own, without the other cards.
