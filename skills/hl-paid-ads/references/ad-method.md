# Ad method

Use this file for the claim check, the policy step, and the variant method.

## Claim types

Each claim type below needs one kind of proof. Without that proof, the line becomes a placeholder.

| Claim type | Proof needed |
|---|---|
| Price or discount | The offer terms, with currency and end date, from the user |
| Count of customers, reviews, or ratings | A dated export or the platform's public page |
| Result or outcome | A case with the user's data, and the date and method |
| Before/after image, or a timed outcome such as "lose X in Y days" or an income figure | Rewrite to a process or capability claim; a timed result needs the user's dated case with its method |
| Superlative or ranking | A dated third-party source that names the ranking, shown on the landing page; without it, the line becomes a placeholder that names the source |
| Health, financial, or legal outcome | The user's compliance review; the skill does not judge it. Check the platform's current policy for any required disclaimer. Flag superlatives, cure or outcome wording, and comparisons with other products; check the current word list of the market's regulator, read in this session, or write `unverified: check the regulator's current word list` |
| Personal attribute of the viewer | The platform's current policy on personal-attribute targeting, read in this session; until then, write `unverified: check the platform's current policy`. The tell is "you" with an attribute such as debt, health, or age. Rewrite to third person or to the condition itself: "Worried about your debt?" becomes "Ways to lower a monthly loan payment." |
| Press quote or third-party logo | The real coverage, dated, from the user. A logo also needs the outlet's logo-use terms, read in this session, or write `unverified: check the outlet's logo-use terms`. Without the coverage, the line becomes a placeholder |
| Guarantee or refund | The written terms from the user: the timeframe, the condition, and what the buyer gets back. The line states the condition, and the business must honor it |
| Dramatized or fake-format ad, such as a scripted chat or a fake AI answer | Mark the concept as dramatized inside the ad copy, in words the viewer sees. Never show it as a real customer, a real AI answer, or a real system message. Do not draft fake reviews or fake system alerts. Check the platform's current policy on such formats, read in this session; until then, write `unverified: check the platform's current policy` |

## Policy step

1. Name the platform the user will use. Do not switch to another platform.
2. Read that platform's current ad policy page in this session, and record its name and the date you read it.
3. Compare each line with the policy's restricted categories. Quote the category name in the Next step when a line may conflict.
4. If the policy page cannot be read, write `unverified: check the platform's current policy` next to each affected line. Do not state a policy from memory.
5. Declare special ad categories. If a line concerns a category the platform treats as special, tell the user to declare that category before the ad runs. Read the platform's current list and rules in this session, including what a declaration changes (targeting limits, and whether it can change after creation). Check the ad text against the same list. For employment or financial ads, check the platform's current rules on pay wording and on role wording such as "now hiring".
6. Give each flag a narrower rewrite. Mark each rewrite `unverified: check the platform's current policy` unless you read that policy page in this session. A rewrite keeps the opening line's angle; removing the opening line makes a different ad.

## Variant method

- One message per variant. A variant that carries two offers splits into two variants.
- Use the field lengths the platform's current policy page states. Without that page, write `unverified: check field length`.
- Name the offer and the price with their currency, as the user supplied them.
- Pick the opening line by the reader's awareness stage, when the brief or the search context shows it and the brief gives no landing page headline. Unaware: a contrarian or pattern-breaking line, or a feeling from one specific moment. Problem-aware: a question in the reader's own words, or a named friction with the outcome it blocks. Solution-aware: proof, a contrast with the usual approach, a state change (the current workflow and the proposed one, each with a concrete artifact), or a documented statistic with its source, population, and period. Product-aware: specific proof of the offer. Most-aware: the offer with a reason to act now. Unknown stage: lead with the offer.
- Put the opening line inside the visible part of the primary text. Write a short version where the opening line lands, and a longer version for readers who expand. Use the platform's stated visible length; without it, write `unverified: check field length`.
- Search copy answers a query; social copy stops a scroll. Do not paste a social opening line into a search headline. Compress it to the claim.
- Search copy takes its keywords only from supplied keyword research, with the match type that research gives each keyword; write `match type unknown` where it gives none. If the user has no keyword research, suggest `hl-keyword-research`.
- Search headlines and other components the platform assembles: if the platform's current documentation, read in this session, combines them, write interchangeable groups (keyword or opening line, benefit, proof, call to action) that all serve the variant's one message, and check that any combination reads as a sentence.
- Adapt copy per platform by rewriting it for that platform's reader and placement. Do not copy one text across platforms.
- Vary the angle or the proof between variants, except follow-ups to a named winning ad. A changed word is not a new variant. Audience wording is not a variant; the skill writes no audience settings. Under broad targeting the opening line may decide who sees the ad; check the platform's current targeting guidance, read in this session, before you state that effect.
- For a batch of static ads (several variants in one request), stay within the requested count: write the core angles first, then give the remaining variants to the one or two angles ranked highest under "Rank each angle". Where the brief supplies the photo or comment, make one variant a native creative type (see [ad-formats.md](ad-formats.md)). Remove near-duplicates before delivery.
- A secondary field complements the headline: it adds proof, answers one objection, or restates the offer in new words. It does not repeat the headline.
- For retargeting copy, name the earlier touch it follows in your notes to the user, not in the ad text. Do not imply knowledge of the viewer's personal data.
- Keep one call to action per variant. Choose its strength by stage: a soft action, such as reading a guide, for an unaware or problem-aware reader; a direct action, such as booking or buying, for a most-aware reader. For the stages between, name the next step the reader can take.
- Call ads run only in hours when a person answers the phone. Until the owner supplies those hours, write `[PLACEHOLDER: hours a person answers the phone]`.
- When the user runs the variants as a test, change one element per test, in this order of impact (heuristic default): angle, opening line or headline, body text, call to action.
- When the user runs a variant as a test, write its hypothesis as one sentence: the metric, the comparison, and the expected direction. Give a magnitude only from the user's own baseline. For the full test design, suggest `hl-ab-test-plan`. Judge the result by the qualified outcome, such as a lead that fits the offer, not by click rate alone.
- When the user supplies results of past variants, name the winning and losing patterns from that export only, keep the winning angle or creative type, and retire a losing angle only by the rule under "Judge the angle". Compute no rate the export does not show.
- Rank each angle by its evidence: the user's own converting ad, then the customer's verbatim language, then a competitor's angle with a dated source that shows the competitor runs it (adapt the angle, never copy the ad), then a team hunch. A hunch gets a test before a full run.
- Judge the angle, not one execution: several failed executions of one angle retire the angle; one failed execution says only that execution failed.
- When the brief supplies customer phrases or ad-comment objections, quote them verbatim, each with its source, date, and use limit (the channels and period the source permits), and build the opening line or the proof from them. A landing page headline in the brief sets the opening line instead (SKILL.md draft step 2). Do not paraphrase them into marketing voice. Quote a customer only when the brief shows permission to quote; otherwise use `[PLACEHOLDER: permission to quote]`. A customer complaint shows the reader's language and pain; it does not prove a performance claim.

## Self-check

- Each figure has a source line in the brief, or it is a placeholder. Keep its population, period, and qualifier in the same line, at the precision the source gives.
- No line promises a result, a rank, or a guaranteed outcome.
- No line names or implies a competitor's weakness without a dated source. Flag a competitor's brand name in the copy; keep it only with a dated source.
- No line depends on a currency the user did not supply.
- The offer, price, and terms in the ad match the landing page exactly, and the page shows them if you read the page in this session; otherwise add a Next step that checks the match.
- No excess capitals, repeated punctuation, or vague call to action.
- Check the display URL, and the safe area of each static crop (the part of the image the platform's interface does not cover), against the platform's current policy page or placement specifications, read in this session; without that read, write `unverified: check the platform's current policy`.
