# Ad calibration

Use this file in draft step 5 of [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) to rate each line. The examples use an invented product, Tidewater Bike Tune, a mobile bicycle service. They are not real claims.

There is no numeric score. Rate each line on the six dimensions below, name its weakest dimension, and rewrite that dimension first. Then re-check the line.

## Dimensions

1. **Specific.** A reader can picture the thing offered.
   - Weak: "Great bike service for everyone."
   - Strong: "Bike tune-up at your door, [PLACEHOLDER: visit length from the user]."
   - Reason: the weak line names no act; the strong line names the service, and its length waits for a source.

2. **Provable.** Each claim has a source line in the brief, or it is a placeholder.
   - Weak: "The best bike mechanic in town."
   - Strong: "Tune-ups priced at [PLACEHOLDER: price with currency from the user]."
   - Reason: "best" is a superlative with no proof; a price with its source can be checked.

3. **Platform fit.** The wording suits the field and the reader's situation: a search answers a query, a social line stops a scroll.
   - Weak, search headline: "Passionate about bikes and our community!"
   - Strong, search headline: "Mobile Bike Tune-Up."
   - Reason: the weak line is a social mood; the strong line uses the words a searcher types.

4. **Landing match.** The ad's opening line matches the landing page headline.
   - Weak: ad "Never ride a squeaky bike again?", page headline "Mobile bike service."
   - Strong: ad and page headline both "Mobile bike tune-up at your door."
   - Reason: the strong pair lets the reader see the promise kept on arrival.

5. **Policy clean.** No claim type the platform commonly restricts, and no tell of a personal attribute.
   - Weak: "Are you struggling to afford repairs?"
   - Strong: "Repair costs itemized before any work starts."
   - Reason: the weak line uses "you" with a financial attribute; the strong line describes the service.

6. **Plain voice.** A person wrote the line for this product. Across the variants, vary the sentence shapes and openings, except that a variant keeps the opening of its landing page headline.
   - Weak: "Not just a tune-up: a full reset, and your bike will feel new."
   - Strong: "Tidewater Bike Tune: [PLACEHOLDER: task list from the user], done at your door."
   - Reason: the weak line uses a colon reveal and a trailing clause, and its claim has no source; the strong line names the service and waits for the task list.
   - Tells to remove: a denial before the real claim, a colon that reveals the payoff, a row of short negations, a question the writer answers, a stock phrase, a generic opener such as "POV:" or "Stop scrolling", and a call to action that fits any product, such as "Get started today".
