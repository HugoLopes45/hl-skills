# Coding scheme

Read this file before coding deals and before writing the Next lines of a readout.

## Reason codes

Code from the buyer's words. Use the CRM reason only when no note or transcript covers the deal, and label that deal `CRM only`.

| Code | Use when | Decision it informs |
|---|---|---|
| Product capability | A capability the buyer named, present or missing, decided the outcome. | Product backlog, with the deal count. A battle card claim needs proof; route it to `hl-positioning`. |
| Price or value | The buyer named price, budget, or value for the price. Note which one. | Pricing review in `hl-pricing`, with the value the buyer named. Not a discount decision. |
| Setup effort | Implementation, time to first use, or services decided it. | Onboarding review in `hl-onboarding`; a discovery question on time to first use. |
| Trust or risk | Security, references, vendor size, or stability decided it. | List the proof that exists. Claims go to `hl-positioning`. |
| Buying terms | Payment, deposit, trial length, contract term, or procurement steps decided it. | A discovery question on those steps; the battle card's where-they-are-strong section when a rival wins on terms. |
| Relationship | An incumbent relationship, a champion who left, or an internal sponsor decided it. | A discovery question on who decides and who advocates. No product change. |
| No decision | The buyer bought nothing. | Sales process follow-up. Not a competitive finding. |
| Built in-house | The buyer built or kept its own method. | The status quo in `hl-positioning`. Not a rival card. |
| Unclear | No note and no CRM reason, words that name no reason, or a bare "price" with no specifics. | None. Collect the note or transcript. |

For a win, the decision is the battle card's where-we-win section, after the same proof check as above.

## Interview questions

Run interviews soon after the decision, while the buyer still remembers the evaluation. The owner sets the window. Use an interviewer other than the deal's rep when possible; if the rep ran it, say so in the readout. Print these questions and the "why" rule in the Next step that asks for interviews; the reader cannot open this file.

- Describe how the search started. What prompted it?
- Name every option you compared. How did the short list form?
- Which two or three criteria carried the decision at the end?
- What did we do well, and where did we fall short?
- At what moment did the choice turn? What happened there?
- For a lost deal: what would have changed your choice at the price you were quoted?

Ask "why" about any answer that stops at price or at a general word such as "better", and repeat it until the buyer names what it cost them, what changed in their choice, or what was at risk, or has nothing to add. Do not ask a yes or no question about a changed price; a polite lost buyer tends to answer yes (heuristic, not sourced).
