# Email calibration

Use with draft step 6 in `SKILL.md`. Each dimension shows a weak and a strong line for one invented product, the Brindle Tea Kettle, a kettle with a hold timer. Every product detail and line here is invented; none is a fact for the user's brief.

1. **Subject.**
   Weak: "Big news from Brindle!"
   Strong: "Brindle firmware 2.1 fixes the hold timer"
   Why: the weak line names a mood; the strong line names what changed.

2. **Preview.**
   Weak: "Read on for the details."
   Strong: "Kettles on firmware 2.0 drop the hold after 10 minutes."
   Why: the weak line adds no fact; the strong line adds the affected group and the symptom, which the subject does not carry.

3. **Opening.**
   Weak: "We hope this email finds you well."
   Strong: "You get this note because your Brindle kettle is registered on firmware 2.0."
   Why: the weak line is a stock phrase any company could send; the strong line states the reason for the email.
   Cold prospect, weak: "Saw you run a cafe, so I thought of Brindle."
   Cold prospect, strong: "[PLACEHOLDER: problem seen at this prospect's cafe, with its source and date]."
   Why: the weak line names no problem; the strong line leads into the problem and marks the source the sender must cite.

4. **Proof.**
   Weak: "Thousands of tea lovers swear by it."
   Strong: "Hold timer test: [PLACEHOLDER: test length and date]."
   Why: the weak line claims a result the brief does not support; the strong line marks the proof the sender must supply.

5. **Call to action.**
   Weak: "Click here now to learn more and upgrade today!"
   Strong: "Install firmware 2.1: [PLACEHOLDER: update page link]"
   Why: the weak line asks for three things and adds pressure with no date; the strong line asks for one action with one link placeholder.

6. **Sign-off and footer.**
   Weak: "Cheers, the Brindle team"
   Strong: "[PLACEHOLDER: sender name], [PLACEHOLDER: role]" with the footer placeholders for a marketing message.
   Why: the weak line hides the sender; the strong line names the slots the user fills with a real sender.

7. **Audience.**
   Weak, for an executive: "Brindle firmware 2.1 extends the hold-timer orchestration layer."
   Strong, for an executive: "Brindle firmware 2.1 stops hold-timer faults on [PLACEHOLDER: fleet size] kettles."
   Why: the weak line uses internal vocabulary and gives no outcome; the strong line states the one outcome an executive weighs. For a technical reader, state the exact behavior, such as the reset condition, instead of the outcome.

Check each dimension in the order above. Rewrite the weakest dimension first, then check again. Stop when no dimension reads weak, or when the only weakness is a proof placeholder the user must fill.
