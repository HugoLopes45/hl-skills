# Format checklists

Read this file when building the run of show or owner checklist. Each row is one task, its owner role, and its done-when. Dates are offsets from the event date. No offset here is a benchmark.

## Every format

| Task | Owner role | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| Write the qualified-meeting definition and the readout | event lead | both are saved before the first invitation |
| Pull the target-buyer list from the organizer breakdown | event lead | the count matches the breakdown |
| Confirm recording and republishing rights in writing | owner's adviser | the answer is written, or the step waits |
| Name the owner of each follow-up tier | meetings owner | each tier has one named role |
| Set the capture method and the source tag | ops or CRM admin | a test contact shows the tag |

## Webinar (host)

1. Decide the topic and one offer before the registration page. The topic serves the target buyer, not existing customers only. Order the content: a few useful points, then a demo where the topic needs one, then the one offer. Do not plan a pitch-only webinar.
2. Write the page headline from the promise, and ask only the fields the follow-up uses. Add one qualifying question only if the follow-up uses its answer.
3. Start promotion two weeks before the date (heuristic default). Send these list messages: the announcement, one preview insight, and a last call. Speakers promote to their own audiences too. Reminder sends go only to people who agreed to that message type.
4. Send a calendar entry at registration. Send reminders one day, one hour, and five minutes before start (heuristic default); the last one carries the join link.
5. Name a moderator and a backup presenter. Say at the open that an offer comes at the end. Script the offer transition word for word. Give one offer with its true reason, such as a real deadline or cohort start, and restate its link during Q&A and at the close.
6. Vary the content at intervals the owner sets, such as a poll or a demo. Say at the open when the content ends.
7. Choose the replay to match the goal: time-limited for a launch or offer, evergreen for education-led lead capture. Any deadline stated in the offer must be true.
8. After the event, use the attendance list. Tier each attendee with `follow-up.md`. Reply within the follow-up deadline to each attendee who asked a question, citing that question. Attendees who left early get the replay with the time they missed. No-shows and replay registrants get one owner-approved replay message, or none.

## Meetup (host)

1. Keep the same date, venue, and format for each run. Name a host rota.
2. Owner invites target-buyer contacts who agreed to be contacted.
3. Check the venue's capacity limit and house rules before invitations.
4. Facilitate a problem the buyers share. Do not pitch a product on the agenda.
5. Put the consent wording on the sign-in sheet, next to the sign-in field.
6. After the event, tier the contacts with `follow-up.md`.

## Workshop (host)

1. Set one hands-on task and a seat count the room allows.
2. List each material with its cost line.
3. Give each attendee an output to keep, such as a checklist or a worked example.
4. Use the attendee's output as the context for the follow-up message.

## Booth (sponsor or exhibitor)

1. Owner books meetings with target buyers before the event. Use a booking link to a calendar, not a form.
2. Write one qualifying question for booth staff, sorted by the qualified-meeting definition.
3. Staff the booth with people who can qualify and demo. Give non-target visitors a quick, polite exit. Write a staff shift plan with breaks.
4. Get these terms in writing before negotiating price: a speaking or panel slot, side-event rights, the attendee data the organizer can deliver, and booth placement.
5. Treat a badge scan as a contact, not a lead. A scan becomes a lead only after a conversation.
6. If the owner plans a side event, the owner invites a curated list of target buyers who agreed to be contacted, with no pitch.

## Dinner (host)

1. Invite only target buyers who agreed to be contacted.
2. Set the seat count. Heuristic default: 8 to 14 guests, set by the owner.
3. Put no product pitch on the agenda.
4. Owner sets the invitation lead time.

## Virtual summit (host)

1. Each speaker promotes to their own audience, with a link and pre-written posts from the event lead.
2. Confirm each speaker's recording and republishing rights in writing.
3. Owner sets each speaker's promotion dates.

## Public demo (host)

1. Demonstrate one real task from start to finish, with no pitch on the agenda.
2. Put the consent wording on the sign-in sheet, next to the sign-in field.

## Live audio session (host)

1. Set one topic the target buyers share, with its end time and a question slot.
2. Record contacts only from people who agreed to be contacted, and show the consent wording where they join.

## Attendance (no booth or stage)

1. List the named target contacts from the speaker and attendee lists. Heuristic default: 15 to 30 contacts.
2. Announce the owner's presence to the owner's audience with the reason to meet.
3. The owner requests a slot with each contact, at a stated time and place. Heuristic default: 15 minutes, set by the owner.
4. Go to the sessions where target contacts speak, ask a real question, and follow up on it.
5. Record context after each conversation: what they said, what they need, and the agreed step.
6. Use the tiers in `follow-up.md`.

## Speaking (speaker)

1. Pitch the audience takeaway, not the company. The product appears as evidence inside the talk, not as its subject.
2. Check this event's call-for-proposals deadline. Title the talk with the outcome, the audience, and a number or tension.
3. Write the abstract as the audience's problem, the two or three things they will know after, and the owner's proof. Keep it short. Heuristic default: under 150 words.
4. A recording of a smaller talk is proof for a larger call. Use it when the call is for a bigger stage.
5. Write the outline before the slides: the listener, one action for their next working day, the win the owner needs, and the blocks. Each block needs a proof; a block with no proof is cut.
6. Say the key numbers and the category name aloud.
7. Before the owner accepts the slot, confirm in writing that the owner will receive the recording and may republish it, as in every format.
8. After the talk, follow up with each person who asked a question or approached the owner, within the follow-up deadline in `follow-up.md`.

## Run of show

One row per step: time or step, action, owner role.

| Time or step | Action | Owner role |
|---|---|---|
| owner sets | send the invitation | event lead |
| event day, before start | check the room, the materials, and the sign-in list | event lead |
| event, after the session | record context for each conversation | presenter |
