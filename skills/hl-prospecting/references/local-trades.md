# Local trades

Read when the segment is one trade in one place. Every rule in `SKILL.md` still applies.

## Choose the trade

If the user has not picked a trade, compare the candidates on three checks, then recommend one: the value of one job, whether the need is urgent or recurring, and whether one owner decides the marketing. Mark a trade where most locations are franchises as needing the owner's decision.

## Define the place

Name the edges of the place: a town, a postal area, or one side of a city. A large city needs narrowing before the list. Use the place name in every search phrasing a customer would type.

## Gather the businesses

- License lists name the license holder, often the owner. Coverage varies by place and trade; some trades are not licensed at all.
- Run several search phrasings a customer would type. Record each name, site, and phone.
- Directory and association lists hold self-selected members. Say so in the list.
- Read profiles and reviews by hand, one at a time. Do not bulk-extract from a map or review site. Check its current terms first.

## Check the site

Search the exact business name and place before you classify a site as `none`. A search with no result is not proof of no site on its own.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| none | No standalone site after an exact-name search and the listing's website field. |
| social-only | Only social pages, a booking page, or a marketplace page. |
| weak | Broken, blank, or slow on a phone; out of date; or no phone number on the first screen. |
| adequate | Loads on a phone, names the service and the place, and shows a way to make contact. |

## Set the baseline

Take the three most-reviewed local businesses in the place, national brands excluded. Record their median rating, median review count, and recent review pace. Compare each candidate with this baseline, never with an absolute number: a review count that is large in a small town can be small in a city.

## Count visible size signals

Count only signals a stranger can find, each with its source and date: review volume near the baseline; ads running on the date checked; more than one location; a visible crew; a current job post; years in business shown; financing or a maintenance plan on the site. An unseen signal counts as zero. The owner sets the cut-offs for high, medium, and low tiers (`Default: none`).

Never estimate a candidate's revenue or call volume. The pitch uses the owner's own numbers.

## Name the leak

Classify each candidate's lost customer by stage:
- `reached`: the site is down, the phone number is not on the first screen, or the ad's promise is missing from the page the ad opens.
- `chosen`: the rating or review count is well under the baseline, reviews are left unanswered, or no new review appears in the period checked.
- `found`: the business is absent from the search results and assistant answers you checked. Name each place checked.

Pick the stage nearest the phone for the candidate's one problem. State it in the owner's words, with the evidence and its date. Report the most common stage across the list.

## Park

- Recent reviews complain about the work itself. Write "fix the work first."
- Franchise locations, unless the offer sells to franchisees.

## Check before contact

Re-check a sample of rows against their sources, and name the rows checked. One failed row sends the whole sample back for review.
