# Market maturity

Use this file in workflow step 8. Awareness and maturity are separate. Awareness is what the buyer knows: the problem, that a solution exists, that this product exists. Maturity is how many similar claims the buyer has already heard. Awareness sets how much the lead must explain. Maturity sets which kind of claim leads.

## Call the maturity per segment

Read the signals in this order, strongest first:

1. **Paid claims.** The ads the alternatives run, as supplied. Ads show what is said now. Compare the homepages with them, because the two can differ.
2. **Complaints.** Buyer phrases such as "they all say that" or "I've tried this before".
3. **Proof on the first screen.** Posted prices, numbered guarantees, named methods and studies on the alternatives' first screens.
4. **AI answers.** Results from [research.md](research.md).

When the signals conflict, mark the call `assumed` and say which signal decided it. When a signal is missing and no other signal decides the call, mark it `unknown`.

## Levels and the lead each calls for

1. **New claim.** Buyers did not know a solution was possible. Lead with the plain promise. Skip comparisons; nothing exists to compare with yet.
2. **Escalating claims.** Rivals make similar promises with "fastest", "#1" or bigger numbers. Lead with a specific, measured promise that states its unit. Skip superlatives.
3. **Claims fatigue.** Ads sound alike, and buyers say "I've tried things like this". Lead with the named method that shows how the promise is kept. Skip bare speed or transformation claims.
4. **Proof race.** Rivals each claim a method, and ads lead with studies, numbers, posted prices or guarantee terms. Lead with proof density and a method the buyer can see. Skip any claim without proof.
5. **Identity.** Every rival has claimed and proven its claims, and one known brand is the default. Lead with who the buyer is, or with the problem the default brand stands for. Skip generic promises.

## Lead by trigger

Use these to pick an opening inside a level. The level's lead and its skips win; a trigger only picks the opening within them. Each trigger needs its evidence; without it, skip that trigger.

- Complaints blame one shared practice or cost --> enemy lead: name that practice in the buyers' words. Never name a rival.
- Buyers reject the standard approach in their own words --> contrarian lead: name the standard approach and the step that differs, with proof.
- The top repeated fear is risk, and the owner keeps a guarantee offered today --> risk reversal: lead with that guarantee, with its number and window.
- Enemy and contrarian leads need an existing standard approach or practice: skip them at level 1.

## Narrow the category

Narrow the category until it names the segment's job. A narrower category is newer to the buyer. Invented example: "invoicing for bakeries that deliver on account" is newer than "invoicing".

Check that no rival already uses a method's name before you claim the name. A real method named first is hard for rivals to take back.
