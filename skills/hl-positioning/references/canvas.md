# Canvas method

Use this file for the detailed steps. The workflow, evidence rules, and output are in `SKILL.md`.

## Canvas fields

Write one row per item the evidence supports. Do not add rows to fill a layout.

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Alternative | What buyers use now, the status quo included; source |
| Attribute | A fact about the product the buyer can check; proof status |
| Benefit | What the attribute does for the buyer |
| Outcome | The result the buyer wants, in the buyer's words where supplied |
| Segment | The buyers who value the attribute most; `assumed` unless evidenced |
| Category | The buyer's frame of reference; why it clarifies the supported value; evidence or hypothesis |

Keep the alternative in the buyer's terms. "Spreadsheet and a weekly reminder" is an alternative; "competitor X" alone does not say what the buyer does.

## Decision summary

Discover the position through alternatives, supported attributes, value, segment and category before summarizing it. A fill-in-the-blanks sentence cannot establish any of those choices.

When useful or requested, summarize the supported position in plain language. No fixed sentence form is required. Once the chain is complete, the statement may take this form: "For [segment] who [situation], [product] is the [category] that [value]; unlike [primary alternative], we [proof attribute]." Keep decisive trade-offs and unknowns visible rather than squeezing them into a slogan. An unsupported choice remains `Hypothesis`, however polished its wording.

## Distinctiveness test

1. Name the alternative from the canvas that is closest to the statement.
2. Ask whether that alternative could honestly write the same sentence. If it could, the statement needs a different attribute, a narrower segment, or a condition.
3. Check each comparative word ("faster", "only", "best") against the proof status. A word with `proof needed` stays out of the statement.

## Proof status

- `proof supplied`: the user gave a document, a demo, or a measured result with its method and date.
- `proof needed`: nothing supports the attribute yet. Write `[PLACEHOLDER: proof needed]` in the canvas.
- `not distinctive`: competitors offer it too, or the buyer does not value it. Keep it out of the statement.

## Scope limits

- A positioning is not a message, a price, or a channel plan. Name the neighbouring skill and stop there.
- Wording may differ by market and language. Write one statement per market only when the evidence for each market differs.
