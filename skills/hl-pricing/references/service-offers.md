# Local Trade and Service Offers

Read this when the business is a local trade or service business and the offer includes a call-out fee, quote levels, a maintenance plan, add-ons, or a guarantee.

Each option below is a test, not a recommendation. Compare each option in the supplied job records: past jobs with their price, cost, and outcome. A comparison of past jobs does not show cause; state an effect only after a test that changes one variable. Without job records, label the option `Hypothesis` and list the missing records in Next.

## Options

- **Call-out fee credited to the job.** The visit fee is deducted from the job price when the customer books the work. Compare the booking rate and the average job price for jobs with and without the credit. Check that the fee covers the cost of a visit that does not convert to a job.
- **Three quote levels.** Offer one job at three price levels, such as basic, standard, and complete. Three is a heuristic default, not a rule. Compare the share of quotes that take each level and the margin on each. Each level needs its own cost floor.
- **Maintenance plan.** A recurring agreement for scheduled visits, billed in advance. Compare the repeat-visit rate and the cost per visit for plan members and other customers. Set the plan price so the included visits clear the measured cost per visit. If the cost per visit is unknown, the plan price stays unresolved.
- **Add-ons before discounts.** Offer optional extra work or upgrades on the quote before any price cut. Compare the share of quotes that accept an add-on, and its margin, with the share that accept a discount, and the margin each discount gives up. A discount needs a start and end date (see [price-changes.md](price-changes.md)).

## Retainers and coaching

- For a retainer or a coaching engagement, the offer names its renewal point: the date, or the delivered result, at which the buyer decides whether to continue. It also names what the buyer keeps if they stop. The owner states both. Without either, write `[PLACEHOLDER: renewal point]` or `[PLACEHOLDER: kept on stop]` in its place and ask the owner for it in Next.

## Guarantees

A guarantee is risk reversal: it moves the cost of a failed promise from the buyer to the seller. Common forms are an arrival window, a workmanship guarantee, a clean site, and no fix, no fee.

- List a guarantee only after the owner confirms that it can be honored and what it costs per job, such as a return visit or an unpaid visit.
- In Next, ask the owner to have an adviser check the terms, conditions, and enforceability of each guarantee. Do not draft them as final terms.
- In Next, ask the owner to have an adviser check the consumer rules for the market before publishing a guarantee, a no fix, no fee rule, or a call-out fee.
- Place a listed guarantee beside its call to action (see [pricing-page.md](pricing-page.md)).
- If the job records show return visits, compare the return-visit rate and cost per job before and after listing the guarantee. A change over time alone does not show that the guarantee caused it.
