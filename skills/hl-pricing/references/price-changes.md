# Price Changes and Discounts

Read this when setting the first price, raising prices, or offering a discount.

## First price

Treat the first price as a bet to learn from. Take the first price from the candidate range in step 4 of [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md), rounded to a round number inside that range. Label it `Hypothesis` until buyer evidence or a tested result supports it.

Do not pick the lowest price to reduce friction. A low price is a hypothesis about buyer quality and later increases, not a fact: check retention by price point and the ease of a later increase in your own data before you decide.

## Signs that a price is too low

Treat these as checks, not thresholds. A number is valid only when it comes from the supplied evidence.

- Buyers do not object to the price in sales or support conversations.
- The product changed since the price was set, and the price did not.
- The cost to serve rose, so the cost floor moved.

A competitor's dated price change is context. It is not a reason to raise your price.

## Raising an existing price

Rollout options for the owner to weigh:

1. Test the new price on new buyers first, with one changed variable (see [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) step 5).
2. Decide on existing customers only after the owner checks the notice and consent rules for each market (see [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) evidence rules).
3. Move a small share of existing customers first, then expand in waves. A heuristic default for the first wave is 5 to 10 percent.
4. Grandfather an old price as a grace period with an end date, or as a permanent exemption. The owner weighs the revenue each option gives up.
5. Expect churn. Check whether it falls on the least profitable, most support-heavy accounts. If it falls on valuable accounts, pause the next wave and review.
6. Offer a choice that keeps value for the customer: a delayed increase, an increase tied to added value, or a plan change.
7. Draft the reason for the owner's notice, in wording the owner approves. Timing stays with the owner (see [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) evidence rules).

## Discounts

Offer a discount only with a start and end date, marked as a test.

Compare the retention of discounted buyers and full-price buyers in your data before you extend a discount.
