# Asset shapes

Read the section for the asset you draft. Its rules apply on top of the Evidence rules in SKILL.md.

## Sales deck outline

Order, one idea per slide:
1. Problem: the buyer's situation, in the buyer's words from the notes.
2. Cost of the problem: the buyer's own figures with their source, or a placeholder.
3. Approach: how the product differs, using only a supplied difference.
4. Proof: one proof point per slide, tagged.
5. Rollout: the owner's dated steps.
6. Price: the supplied price list, with its date and tax basis.
7. One next step with a date.

- Reorder by buyer role. An economic buyer sees cost and price early. A technical buyer sees approach and proof early. A champion sees the internal message and the rollout early.
- Each slide has a headline that states its one idea. Its body carries source tags.

## One-pager

- Order: problem, solution, differentiators (each tagged), one proof point, one next step with a named contact.
- The contact is a person the owner supplied. Otherwise write `[PLACEHOLDER: named contact]`. Never invent a contact address.
- Champion version: the same supplied facts in the champion's own words from the notes, written for the champion to forward. It adds no claim, and the champion checks every line before forwarding.

## Demo script

- A demo needs discovery notes that hold the buyer's pains in their words. Without them, write the discovery script.
- Each scene has a name, its minutes, the pain it answers (quoted from the notes, tagged), the step it shows, and one question to the buyer.
- Start with the buyer's first pain, not the feature list. End each scene on its question.
- Scene minutes add up to the total length the owner sets. `Default: none`.
- Cut any shown feature that answers no pain in the notes.

## Discovery script

- Use it before any demo, and when no discovery notes exist.
- Cover in order: current process, pains in the buyer's words, cost of the problem in the buyer's own figures, decision process and people, timeline, and an agreed next step with a date.
- Ask questions, not statements. Do not state a cost or a pain the buyer did not give.

## Call opener

- Four parts in order: name and company; a specific reason, tagged to a supplied trigger; a request for permission to continue; and nothing more until the buyer answers.
- Never claim a prior contact that did not happen, and never claim to know the buyer's situation without a source.
- Stop at the first refusal of permission or request to end contact. Address a product objection only while permission to continue remains. Log each refusal reason for the sequence review.
- Call recording and calling-consent rules go to the owner's adviser.

## Live objection

Record first: the speaker and role, the deal stage, the next step, and the exact words. Then write:
1. The likely concern, with the note text that points to it. If two readings fit, one question that tells them apart.
2. One response the rep can say as written: acknowledge, address the concern, give one supplied proof, end with a question.
3. Proof from supplied material only. If none fits, say so and name the claim to verify.
4. When not to fight: if the objection shows a real fit gap, say so and name how to qualify out or narrow the scope.

## Objection sheet

- Group objections by price, timing, competition, authority, status quo, and technical.
- Each objection has: the statement as reps hear it, the concern behind it, one response, the tagged proof or a placeholder, and one follow-up question.
- Remove any response that repeats a rival's claim as fact.

## Battle card

- One rival per card. If the request does not name the rival, write `[PLACEHOLDER: rival name]` and add the rival's supply to Next, with `Default: none`.
- Sections, in order: when reps meet this rival (segments and triggers, from notes or a win-loss readout); where the rival is strong, as its dated capture or buyers state it; where we win and for whom, only with win-loss or call evidence; discovery questions that surface the criteria where we are stronger; top objections with one response each; walk-away profile (deals where the rival fits better, from evidence).
- End each fact with its source and the date it was last verified. A rival fact with no dated capture is a placeholder.
- Keep it to one page. Cut any line no rep uses in a call.

## Value calculator spec

- Inputs: each named with its unit and who supplies it, the prospect or the owner. No input has a default value.
- Formulas: each written out with its inputs, and each input's source beside it.
- Outputs: the saving per period, payback in months, and the total over the period the owner sets. Show an output only when every input it needs is supplied. Otherwise write a placeholder.
- Sample: one row with invented values, labeled `sample`, to show the layout. Never present a sample as a result.

## Case study

- Profile line: industry, size as supplied, and buyer role.
- Challenge and solution: a few sentences each, from the customer's words or the record, tagged.
- Metrics: only the before and after values the records hold, each with its record, date, and method.
- Quote: one quote with a recorded permission for this use. Otherwise a placeholder.
- Tags: industry, use case, company size, and buyer role.

## Proposal

- Executive summary: the buyer's stated challenge (quoted from the notes), the solution in a short paragraph, and an outcome only when a supplied record supports it.
- Solution mapped to each stated need: the need (quoted from the notes), the feature, and the proof tag.
- Plan: dated steps with owners by role, dates set by the owner.
- Investment: the price from the supplied price list, with its date and tax basis, and what is included, as supplied. Keep the price in the body, not in an appendix.
- Next step with a decision date that the buyer agrees to. The owner sets the default date: `Default: none`.

## Playbook outline

- Owner: a role, not a person. The owner sets the interval of the periodic review: `Default: none`.
- Buyer profile from the notes, and qualification criteria written as questions the rep answers. Stage rules belong to `hl-lead-lifecycle`.
- Discovery questions, grouped by topic.
- Objections heard most often, with their count and source, each linked to the objection sheet.
- Competitive positioning: links to the battle cards.
- Demo flow for each buyer role, as a scene order.
- Follow-up notes: an outline only. The owner supplies the wording.
- Review: the people who close deals review the playbook with the owner. Each change records its reason.
