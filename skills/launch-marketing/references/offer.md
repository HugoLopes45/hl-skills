# Offer

Design the offer, pick one acquisition channel and run enough attempts to learn, and check that the unit economics work. Name dollars and rates, not "engagement".

Tier structure and segment pricing belong to `pricing.md`.

## Principles

- Value equation: value = (dream outcome × perceived likelihood of success) / (time to result × effort and sacrifice). To improve an offer, move one term and name it.
- Volume beats luck. One outreach attempt is a coin flip; one hundred give a rate. "I tried it and it did not work" usually means too few attempts.
- Charge for the outcome, not the time.
- Four ways to get customers: warm outreach, cold outreach, paid ads, content. Pick one, reach a stable rate, then add a second.
- A lead is a contactable person who showed interest, not a follower or a view.
- Unit economics first: customer acquisition cost (CAC) against lifetime value (LTV). Payback beyond 12 months means you are financing your customers.
- Bonuses add value against named objections, real scarcity creates urgency, and guarantees reverse risk.
- Repetition with iteration beats cleverness in outreach scripts, ad hooks, and content angles.

## Modes

### `offer` (default)

1. **Dream outcome**: the state change the buyer wants, not a feature. Vague means stop and make it specific.
2. **Perceived likelihood**: proof that it works for this buyer (case study, demo, guarantee, niche testimonial).
3. **Time to result**: how long until the outcome, and what the first win in 7 days is.
4. **Effort**: what the buyer must do. Done for them beats done with them beats do it yourself.
5. **Stack**: bonuses, each tied to a named objection and the term it moves.
6. **Anchor and price**: something comparable the buyer already accepts as expensive, then the price.
7. **Verdict**: `strong` / `not yet, fix <term>` / `wrong buyer`.

### `acquire`

Match the channel to the price point and to where the buyer already is. Check whether the user ran at least 100 attempts on the chosen channel. Define a lead magnet that shows the outcome in five minutes. Funnel math: leads, conversations, close rate, CAC, each measured or labeled estimate. Verdict: one channel, an attempt target, and the next move this week.

### `economics`

Table: CAC, LTV, gross margin, payback period, monthly burn and runway, each marked measured or estimated. Warning lines: LTV:CAC under 3:1, payback over 12 months, gross margin under 50% for software, refunds over 5% (the offer over-promises). The first line states the number that hurts the business most, as a figure (for example `LTV $180 < CAC $250`), and the one move that changes it.

### `constraint`

List what limits growth (sales capacity, support, product limits, delivery, hiring), then pick the single constraint. If acquisition is the constraint, state 10× volume in attempts and money. If the next step needs a hire: who, doing what, paid how, freeing which task. Verdict: optimize or push volume.
