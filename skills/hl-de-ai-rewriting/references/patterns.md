# Patterns

Use this file to flag sentences. A pattern is a reason to look at a sentence, not proof that a person or a model wrote it. Fix the pattern by stating the fact it stood in for. If no fact exists, the sentence is a candidate for removal, and the author decides.

Two kinds of rule apply:

- **Remove or rewrite**: flag every instance.
- **Cap**: inspect repetition for redundancy. Counts are review prompts, not universal limits; keep repetition that serves the meaning or the user's style.

## Padding

- **Empty announcement** (remove or rewrite). "We are excited to share some news." States no fact. Fix: put the fact in the sentence.
- **Vague praise** (review). "This thoughtful change serves our valued community." Check whether it conveys a real benefit or the author's opinion. Preserve first-person opinions as opinions; do not demand a metric for every feeling. Rewrite only empty wording, without inventing a supporting fact.
- **Closing filler** (rewrite). "Please do not hesitate to reach out." Adds no instruction. Fix: state the next step, for example "Call the shop."
- **Stock opener** (remove or rewrite). "In today's fast-paced world." Starts with a claim no reader needs. Fix: open with the subject of the text.
- **Throat clearing** (remove). "It is worth noting that." "The truth is." Delete the phrase; keep the sentence.
- **Recap close** (rewrite). "In summary, the process is simple and effective." Repeats the text. Fix: end on the last concrete point.

## Claims

- **Vague authority** (rewrite). "Experts agree." "Many people believe." Names no source. Fix: name the source the author has, or narrow the claim to what the author observed. If the author has neither, flag it for the author.
- **Stacked hedge** (rewrite). "This may potentially suggest that it could." Three hedges on one claim. Fix: keep one hedge that matches the author's confidence.
- **Inflated importance** (rewrite). "This marks a pivotal turning point." Asserts significance without a reason. Fix: state what changed and for whom.
- **Unearned certainty** (flag). "This always works." "Nothing else compares." Fix: keep the condition the author gave; if none, ask the author for it.

## Structure

- **Negative parallelism** (rewrite, only when the contrast is a rhetorical setup). "It is not just about speed; it is about trust." The sentence asserts both halves, so keep both as claims: state speed and trust in the sentence, with the reason each holds. Remove a half only when the author confirms it is a foil they do not mean. Keep every explicit negation, contrast, or exclusion the author states as a fact, such as "not refundable after 30 days"; these are claims, not patterns.
- **Reflexive triad** (cap: once per section). Three items when the text has two facts. Fix: list the facts the text has.
- **Rhetorical question then answer** (rewrite). "Why does this matter? Because it saves time." Fix: state the reason directly, unless the reader would ask the question.
- **Colon reveal** (rewrite). "The best part: it adapts." Fix: write a normal sentence.
- **Uniform rhythm** (cap). Every sentence has the same length and shape. Fix: vary length only where the content differs; do not add length for its own sake.
- **Fragment drumbeat** (cap: once per section). "Simple. Fast. Reliable." Fix: merge into one sentence with a fact.
- **Connective chain** (cap). "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," in a row. Fix: remove the connective when the order shows the link.

## Vocabulary

Word lists age. Use these as prompts to look at a sentence, not as a ban.

- Stock verbs: "delve," "underscore," "showcase," "foster," "navigate the landscape." Replace with the real verb or with the fact.
- Empty intensifiers: "truly," "genuinely," "significantly," "remarkably." Delete unless the text needs the degree, and then state the degree.
- Abstract nouns used as subjects: "the solution empowers teams." Make the person or the action the subject.

## Formatting

- Bold on every list item or decorative heading in prose. Keep bold only where the reader scans for it.
- Punctuation that obscures sentence structure. Follow the user's style; no punctuation count establishes authorship.
- Emoji as bullets in prose. Remove unless the author uses them as content.

## Self-check

1. Search the text for each pattern above and mark each hit.
2. Inspect repetition for a demonstrated reading problem, not a quota breach.
3. Compare the claim list with the rewrite. Restore any claim, number, condition, or negation that is missing.
4. Confirm that the rewrite adds no fact, name, number, source, or first-person detail.
