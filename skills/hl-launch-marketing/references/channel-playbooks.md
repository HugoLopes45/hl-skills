# Channel Playbooks

Read when a channel in `channels` mode is community, partners, or free tools. Each playbook names its kill signal, the sign to stop.

## Community

- Build around an identity the members want to reinforce. The product brings members in; the shared identity keeps them.
- Give members value before asking anything of them.
- Pick one community model before the first ritual: support-driven when members can answer repeat questions, product-development when technical users shape the roadmap, education when activation depends on skill, or founder-led until the team can run it.
- Recruit the first members by hand, about 20 to 50 (heuristic default), before opening the community to the public.
- Name one weekly action members repeat, and reward it publicly.
- Change your role as the community grows: architect it first, then run rituals and moderation, then let members run sub-groups and advocate work.
- If members answer each other's questions, turn the top questions into a searchable answer list, credit members when you ship what they asked for, and check complaints weekly for signs of churn.
- Find advocates from unprompted recommendations, ask each one personally and say why you chose them, and give talking points and a referral link. Track their sign-ups; route reward rules to `hl-referral-program`.
- Before a community or answer-site play, read that site's current promotion rule and cite it; start with value posts.
- Track the share of new members who post in their first week, questions unanswered for a day or more (heuristic default), the share of posts from members rather than staff, the share of members who joined but did not post in 30 days (heuristic default), and whether a few members supply most replies.
- Kill signal: the team writes most posts, or new members stop posting after their introduction.

## Partners

- Name the partner type first: reseller or co-marketing. A reseller partner earns margin by selling and supporting the product; co-marketing puts your asset in front of the partner's audience. Integrations and marketplace listings belong to the marketplaces and integrations channel; referral and affiliate programs are out of scope here.
- Rank candidates by audience fit, reciprocity, and past co-marketing; the ranking orders them, it does not qualify them.
- Check the audience overlap before pitching.
- Give the partner something useful first, such as a finished asset for their audience, before asking for their time.
- Before asking a partner for time, check whether an asset you publish alone can reach a bigger brand's audience; check trademark and platform rules before using another brand's name.
- Bring two or three concrete campaign ideas to the first call; "let's work together" moves no partner.
- For each partner, state what they get in return: margin, audience reach, shared leads or a fee; agree it before the first call.
- Pick each campaign's format with its lead rule before the work starts: co-authored post (shared byline), joint guide (gated; leads split only for people who consented at capture), newsletter swap (each side keeps its leads), or joint giveaway (list building). Offer your lead magnet to the partner's audience as the shared asset.
- A reseller program depends on enablement: onboarding, certification, sales material, deal registration, and a co-selling motion. Sign partners only after you can name who trains them and what they sell.
- Before the campaign, agree on lead ownership, each side's promotion duties, who approves assets, the timeline, who pays for what, which logos and brand assets each side may use, and which metrics each side shares afterward. Name the shared channel and its owner before the first call.
- Kill signal: no reply after the agreed follow-up, or lead ownership still undefined.

## Free tools

- Build only when buyers search for a job they already pay for elsewhere, and the tool leads to the product.
- Check search demand, buyer match, what similar free tools already do, and maintenance load before building. Compare expected leads times lead value with build and upkeep cost, and state what your version does better.
- Keep the first version to one job, with clear input, clear output, and working mobile use.
- Choose the tool type from the buyer's job: calculator for numbers, analyzer for existing work, generator for quick output, interactive for learning.
- Build custom only for a core, unique tool; embed an existing tool when it is not your differentiator.
- Name the job, the product step it leads to, and the search its landing page targets (for example, "[thing] calculator"); check whether others would link to it.
- A microsite needs its own search check and its own lead tag.
- Track leads and sign-ups from the tool, not visits.
- Budget upkeep for any tool that takes user input or calls outside services. Such tools rot and become attack surfaces.
- Kill signal: sign-ups stay flat through a measured promotion test.
